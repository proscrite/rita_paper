\section{Experimental apparatus}
\label{sec:experiment}

This section summarizes the hardware used to produce and record the alpha‑tagged recoil events
analyzed in this work. A photographic overview of the key elements is shown in
Figure~\ref{fig:setup_photo}.

\subsection{RITA chamber and gas system}
The RITA apparatus is a small time‑projection chamber (TPC) using gas xenon as active detection
medium. The main component in the setup is the alpha source, which consists of silicon detector
with an extremely diluted layer of \isotope{Th}{228} deposited on its active surface. The shielding
of the detector is grounded and acts as cathode. A conceptual illustration of this source is shown
in Fig. \ref{fig:setup_schematic}: The \isotope{Th}{228} atoms sit on the surface of the silicon.
50\% of the alpha decays are emitted inward and tagged by the detector. The \isotope{Ra}{224} ions
recoil backward into the gas volume and produce a trace of ionization electrons. A homogeneous
electric drift field is applied between the cathode and a grid, acting as a gate. Another grid
behind the gate (anode). The TPC is instrumented with a single photomultiplier tube (PMT) for light
readout The chamber is typically operated with xenon gas at pressures near 2~bar. Typical geometric
parameters used in the analysis are a drift gap of approximately 1.4~cm and an electroluminescence
(EL) gap of approximately 0.8~cm; the EL field (anode voltage divided by EL gap) and the gate
voltage are varied between runs to probe the impact on electron extraction and S2 yield.

The gas handling system (see Fig.~\ref{fig:setup_photo}, left panel) provides continuous
circulation and purification of the xenon and allows the chamber to be evacuated and re‑filled for
the neutralization/collection experiments described in Section~\ref{sec:discussion}. The chamber an
optical window connecting to the PMT chamber.

\subsection{Detectors and readout}
The PMT provides the fast optical readout used to identify the prompt scintillation (S1) and the
delayed electroluminescence (S2) produced when ionization electrons are extracted into the EL
region. Signals are recorded by a fast oscilloscope with multi‑channel acquisition. Typical
sampling rates used in the runs analyzed here are 5~GS/s. The silicon alpha detector is mounted
behind the radioactive coating and records the alpha energy and time for each decay; alpha frames
are correlated with their corresponding PMT frames via a unique identifier (UID) scheme so that
recoils can be tagged by isotope. The silicon alpha detector used for tagging was custom‑built
in‑house (not a commercial unit) and is instrumented to provide a prompt energy measurement with
sufficient resolution to discriminate the \isotope{Th}{228} alpha ROI from nearby satellite peaks;
its output is routed into a single‑channel analyzer (SCA) and timing logic used in the per‑event
gate switching.

\subsection{Source and sample geometry}
The alpha source is a thin \isotope{Th}{228} coating applied to the silicon detector. When an alpha
decay occurs the alpha particle deposits its energy in the silicon while the recoiling daughter ion
is ejected into the xenon gas. Depending on the direction, the ion may travel towards the gate/EL
region where it produces a short ionization track and consequently an S2 signal after drift and EL
amplification. Figure~\ref{fig:setup_schematic} illustrates the basic geometry: the silicon alpha
detector, the gate electrode and the sensor (anode/EL region).
